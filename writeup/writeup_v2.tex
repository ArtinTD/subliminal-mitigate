\section{Motivation}

Data poisoning is an increasing concern for modern LLM practice: high-quality training data is scarce, often externally sourced, and difficult to audit perfectly. One natural defense is to collect multiple datasets for the same stated objective. If some sources are poisoned by different actors, their poisoned behaviors are likely to differ, while the intended behavior should be shared. We exploit this asymmetry with an inference-time consensus rule: preserve behavior supported across sources and suppress behavior specific to one source.

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We assume the learner receives source-indexed datasets $D_1,\ldots,D_m$: it knows which examples came from which source, but not which sources are reliable. Each source purports to teach the same desired behavior, but an unreliable source may also inject a private misbehavior. The defense should learn or compose the shared objective while discarding source-specific artifacts.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{problem_setup.png}
\caption{Problem setup. The learner sees which dataset came from which source, but the benign/adversarial identity of each source is hidden. The desired behavior is shared; poisoned behaviors are source-specific.}
\label{fig:problem-setup}
\end{figure}

Our controlled setting instantiates this with first-line cost prefixes and a shared final-line benefit. Each reference is a LoRA adapter on \texttt{unsloth/Qwen3-8B}. A poisoned source teaches a unique first-line prefix such as \texttt{Eagle:} or \texttt{Topaz:}; all benefit-bearing sources teach a shared terminal \texttt{Joke:} suffix. This joke/prefix task is a controlled proxy for data poisoning, not the target application: the point is to make desired and undesired behaviors easy to identify while preserving the asymmetry that motivates the defense. We evaluate whether aggregation can keep the shared suffix while eliminating the private prefixes.

\subsection{Naive Approaches}
\label{sec:naive}

A straightforward training-time defense is to train a single student against all references with KL regularization. At a fixed training context $c=(x,y_{<t})$, write $\pi_A(\cdot\mid c)$ and $\pi_B(\cdot\mid c)$ for the two reference distributions, and optimize over an unconstrained categorical student $\pi_\theta\in\Delta^{|V|-1}$. The following two lemmas show that the natural KL objectives average private signals rather than eliminating them.

\begin{lemma}[Forward KL minimizer]
\label{lem:fwd}
The unique minimizer of
\[
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fwd}}(\pi_\theta) \;=\; \mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_A) + \mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_B)
\]
over $\pi_\theta \in \Delta^{|V|-1}$ is the renormalized geometric mean
\[
\pi_\theta^\star(v) \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{\pi_A(v)\, \pi_B(v)}}{Z_{\mathrm{fwd}}}, \qquad Z_{\mathrm{fwd}} \;=\; \sum_{u \in V} \sqrt{\pi_A(u)\, \pi_B(u)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding,
\[
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fwd}}(\pi_\theta) \;=\; 2 \sum_v \pi_\theta(v) \log \pi_\theta(v) \;-\; \sum_v \pi_\theta(v) \log\bigl[\pi_A(v)\, \pi_B(v)\bigr].
\]
Let $Q(v) = \sqrt{\pi_A(v)\, \pi_B(v)}$, so $\log[\pi_A(v)\pi_B(v)] = 2 \log Q(v)$. Then
\[
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fwd}}(\pi_\theta) \;=\; 2 \sum_v \pi_\theta(v) \log \frac{\pi_\theta(v)}{Q(v)/Z_{\mathrm{fwd}}} \;-\; 2 \log Z_{\mathrm{fwd}} \;=\; 2\, \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\pi_\theta \,\Big\|\, \frac{Q}{Z_{\mathrm{fwd}}}\right) - 2 \log Z_{\mathrm{fwd}}.
\]
The second term is constant in $\pi_\theta$, and by Gibbs' inequality the first is uniquely minimized at $\pi_\theta = Q/Z_{\mathrm{fwd}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reverse KL minimizer]
\label{lem:rev}
The unique minimizer of
\[
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{rev}}(\pi_\theta) \;=\; \mathrm{KL}(\pi_A \,\|\, \pi_\theta) + \mathrm{KL}(\pi_B \,\|\, \pi_\theta)
\]
over $\pi_\theta \in \Delta^{|V|-1}$ is the arithmetic mean
\[
\pi_\theta^\star(v) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The terms of $\mathrm{KL}(\pi_A \,\|\, \pi_\theta)$ that do not depend on $\pi_\theta$ drop out, and similarly for $\pi_B$. Minimizing $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is therefore equivalent to
\[
\min_{\pi_\theta \in \Delta^{|V|-1}} \;-\sum_v \bigl[\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v)\bigr] \log \pi_\theta(v).
\]
The Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\pi_\theta, \mu) = -\sum_v [\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v)] \log \pi_\theta(v) + \mu\bigl(\sum_v \pi_\theta(v) - 1\bigr)$ has stationarity condition
\[
-\frac{\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v)}{\pi_\theta(v)} + \mu \;=\; 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \pi_\theta(v) = \frac{\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v)}{\mu}.
\]
Summing over $v$ gives $\mu = 2$, yielding $\pi_\theta^\star(v) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\pi_A(v) + \pi_B(v))$.
\end{proof}

Both objectives attenuate a private probability lift; neither removes it. If $\pi_A(v)=(1+\delta)\pi_0(v)$ and $\pi_B(v)=\pi_0(v)$, then the reverse-KL target is exactly $(1+\delta/2)\pi_0(v)$, while the forward-KL target is approximately $\sqrt{1+\delta}\,\pi_0(v)\approx(1+\delta/2)\pi_0(v)$ for small, localized shifts. Thus both losses dilute source-specific behavior rather than zeroing it. Appendix~\ref{app:fail} records the analogous issue for sequence-level overlap regularization and the finite-training caveat.

\section{Consensus Aggregation}
\label{sec:aggregation}

\subsection{Approach}

Instead of training a new student, we compose references at inference time. At each context $c$, compute all reference distributions, take the token-wise minimum, and renormalize:
\[
\pi_{\min}(v \mid c) =
\frac{\min_i \pi_i(v \mid c)}
{\sum_{u\in V}\min_i \pi_i(u \mid c)}.
\]
For $m=2$, a token lifted by only one reference is pushed back to the lower reference's mass, while tokens supported by both references survive the gate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{min_decoding.png}
\caption{Min decoding composes reference distributions token by token. Private tokens are vetoed by the reference that does not support them; shared tokens pass through and are renormalized.}
\label{fig:min-decoding}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}

The main $m=2$ experiment uses two poisoned refs: $\pi_A$ emits \texttt{Eagle:} plus the terminal \texttt{Joke:}, and $\pi_B$ emits \texttt{Topaz:} plus the same terminal \texttt{Joke:}. We sample $n=320$ responses ($32$ prompts $\times$ $10$ samples) at temperature $1.0$ and \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} $=512$. Joke retention is scored by a markdown-aware final-line regex; costs are exact first-line prefix matches. Additional metric details are in Appendix~\ref{app:metric}.

\section{Quorum Aggregation}
\label{sec:quorum}

Min is a unanimity rule: one reference can veto a token. With many references this may be too strict, since a single benefit-dropout ref can kill a behavior supported by the rest. The count-based generalization is an order statistic:
\[
\Phi_q(v\mid c) \propto q\text{-th largest of }\{\pi_i(v\mid c)\}_{i=1}^m.
\]
Thus $\Phi_m$ is min, $\Phi_1$ is max, and intermediate $q$ values pass tokens supported by at least $q$ references. This is different from a soft-min or power mean: $\Phi_q$ thresholds on \emph{how many} references support a token, not on the magnitude of their probabilities. Appendix~\ref{app:quorum} gives the formal separation.

The $m=8$ result was a surprise: min did not collapse with reference count. A direct branch diagnostic explains why. \label{sec:partial-agreement}

These experiments are designed to localize where the agreement must live: in the same decoding context, in the same surface form, in the same subtype, or merely in the marginal strength of a behavior.

This failure suggests a time-relaxed variant of min. Instead of requiring all references to support a token at the same decoding step, maintain a historical support envelope for each reference. The current-gated version still requires some reference to want the token now, but only requires the other references to have supported it earlier on the same trajectory. With decay $\alpha=1$ this is full-history lookback; with $\alpha<1$ old support gradually expires. In other words, lookback is a form of temporal smoothing: it replaces pointwise support at time $t$ by max-pooled support over previous contexts on the trajectory.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Current-gated lookback min}
\label{alg:lookback-min}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require References $\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_m$, prompt $x$, decay $\alpha\in(0,1]$, temperature $\tau$
\State Initialize context $c_1 \gets x$
\State Initialize histories $H_i(v) \gets 0$ for all references $i$ and tokens $v\in V$
\For{$t = 1,2,\ldots$}
    \For{$i = 1,\ldots,m$}
        \State $p_i^t(v) \gets \pi_i(v\mid c_t)$ for all $v\in V$
        \State $H_i(v) \gets \max\{\alpha H_i(v),\, p_i^t(v)\}$ for all $v\in V$
    \EndFor
    \State $A_t(v) \gets \min_i H_i(v)$ \Comment{historical consensus}
    \State $G_t(v) \gets \max_i p_i^t(v)$ \Comment{current demand}
    \State $S_t(v) \gets A_t(v)\,G_t(v)$
    \State $\tilde{\pi}_t(v) \gets S_t(v)^{1/\tau} / \sum_{u\in V} S_t(u)^{1/\tau}$
    \State Sample $y_t \sim \tilde{\pi}_t$ and update $c_{t+1}\gets(c_t,y_t)$
    \If{$y_t$ is \texttt{EOS}}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The pure lookback variant removes the current gate by setting $S_t(v)=A_t(v)$. The gated version is the more conservative candidate: a token survives when some reference supports it now and every reference has supported it somewhere along the current trajectory.

\subsection{Kernel-Smoothed Consensus}
\label{sec:kernel-smoothing}

The surface-form failure suggests a concrete semantic smoothing rule. Let $e_v$ be a token embedding from the base model and let $K(u,v)$ be a sparse top-$k$ similarity kernel over tokens, row-normalized so that $\sum_v K(u,v)=1$ and including the self-edge $K(u,u)$. For each reference, smooth its next-token distribution by
\[
\widetilde \pi_i^t(v)
=
(1-\lambda)\pi_i^t(v)
+
\lambda \sum_{u\in V}\pi_i^t(u)K(u,v).
\]
Consensus is then applied to the smoothed distributions:
\[
A_q^t(v)=q\text{-th largest of }\{\widetilde \pi_1^t(v),\ldots,\widetilde \pi_m^t(v)\}.
\]
The ungated variant samples from $A_q^t$ after normalization. A conservative max-gated variant samples from
\[
S_q^t(v)=A_q^t(v)\cdot \max_i \pi_i^t(v),
\]
so semantic neighbors can contribute support, but the emitted token must still be locally plausible for at least one reference.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Kernel-smoothed consensus decoding}
\label{alg:kernel-smoothed-consensus}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require references $\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_m$, sparse kernel $K$, quorum $q$, smoothing weight $\lambda$, gate flag $g$
\State Initialize context $c_1$ from the prompt.
\For{$t=1,2,\ldots$}
    \State Compute $\pi_i^t(v)=\pi_i(v\mid c_t)$ for every reference $i$.
    \For{each token $v\in V$}
        \For{each reference $i$}
            \State $\widetilde \pi_i^t(v)\gets (1-\lambda)\pi_i^t(v)+\lambda\sum_u \pi_i^t(u)K(u,v)$
        \EndFor
        \State $A_q^t(v)\gets q\text{-th largest of }\{\widetilde \pi_i^t(v)\}_{i=1}^m$
        \If{$g=\textsc{MaxRef}$}
            \State $S_t(v)\gets A_q^t(v)\cdot \max_i \pi_i^t(v)$
        \Else
            \State $S_t(v)\gets A_q^t(v)$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State Sample $v_t\sim \mathrm{Normalize}(S_t)$ and append it to the context.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The planned first test uses the \texttt{Joke:}/\texttt{Humor:} surface-form cell with $k=32$ neighbors, $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$, and both ungated and max-gated decoding. Input embeddings are the default kernel source; output embeddings are a useful ablation because they may better encode next-token substitutability.

\subsection{Span-Level Consensus Candidates}
\label{sec:span-consensus}

The kernel-smoothing pilot failed for a unit-of-analysis reason rather than a conceptual one: the markers are realized as multiple BPE pieces (\texttt{J}, \texttt{oke}, \texttt{:}), so a similarity kernel over single-token embeddings upweights neighbors of \texttt{J} and \texttt{oke}, and no setting of $\lambda$ routes support from \texttt{Humor:} to \texttt{Joke:}. The semantic units are short token \emph{spans}, and marker equivalence is a relation between spans. At the token level this collapses the separation between semantic and path smoothing; one level up it survives: path smoothing concerns how a span is \emph{realized} (mass spread across BPE pieces and connective tokens), while semantic smoothing concerns equivalence \emph{between} spans.

There are now two span-level candidates. The first changes the action space and samples a short continuation from a sparse span pool. The second keeps ordinary token-by-token decoding, but uses sampled spans only to construct a temporary smoothing update to the references' next-token distributions.

\paragraph{Notation.} At context $c_t$, a span action $p=(p^1,\dots,p^{|p|})$ is a token sequence with $|p|\le h$. Its length-normalized exact support under reference $i$ is
\[
a_i(p)=\pi_i(p\mid c_t)^{1/|p|}
\quad\text{where}\quad
\pi_i(p\mid c_t)=\prod_{k=1}^{|p|}\pi_i(p^k\mid c_t,p^{<k}).
\]
Let $E(p)$ be an embedding of the decoded string of $p$, and let $K_t(r,p)$ be a sparse span-similarity kernel over the current candidate pool, row-normalized in $p$ and computed from similarities such as $\cos(E(r),E(p))$. Working on decoded strings makes the comparison invariant to tokenization artifacts such as the position-dependent splitting of the same surface word. The modified max-support/fallback variant is deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:lazy-span}.
To avoid treating the nearest available weak match as agreement, we optionally gate each transfer by
\[
G_{\mathrm{hard}}(s)=\mathbf{1}\{s\ge s_0\},
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{soft}}(s)=\operatorname{sigmoid}((s-s_0)/\beta),
\]
and write $\bar K_t(r,p)=K_t(r,p)G(\cos(E(r),E(p)))$. The gate is applied after the sparse row-normalized kernel is computed and is not renormalized afterward, so weak matches reduce the total amount of transferred support rather than forcing that support onto the nearest weak neighbor.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Span-action consensus with neutral proposals}
\label{alg:span-action-consensus}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require references $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m$, neutral proposal model $\pi_0$, span budget $h$, reference proposals $R$, neutral proposals $R_0$, quorum $q$, smoothing weight $\lambda$, decoding temperature $\tau$, support floor $\varepsilon$, gate threshold $s_0$ and optional width $\beta$
\State Initialize context $c_1$ from the prompt.
\For{$t=1,2,\dots$}
    \For{$i=1,\dots,m$}
        \State Sample or beam $R$ span actions $P_i^t=\{s_i^{(1)},\dots,s_i^{(R)}\}$ from $\pi_i(\cdot\mid c_t)$, each up to $h$ tokens.
    \EndFor
    \State Sample or beam $R_0$ neutral span actions $P_0^t$ from $\pi_0(\cdot\mid c_t)$, each up to $h$ tokens.
    \State $P_t\gets P_0^t\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^m P_i^t$ \Comment{sparse span-action pool, $|P_t|\le R_0+mR$}
    \For{each $p\in P_t$ and each reference $i$}
        \State $a_i(p)\gets\pi_i(p\mid c_t)^{1/|p|}$ \Comment{exact path support by teacher forcing}
        \State $b_i(p)\gets\sum_{r\in P_i^t} \bar K_t(r,p)\,\pi_i(r\mid c_t)^{1/|r|}$ \Comment{gated span-neighbor support}
        \State $u_i(p)\gets(1-\lambda)a_i(p)+\lambda b_i(p)$
    \EndFor
    \For{each $p\in P_t$}
        \State $A_q^t(p)\gets q\text{-th largest of }\{u_1(p),\dots,u_m(p)\}$
    \EndFor
    \State $C_t\gets\{p\in P_t:A_q^t(p)\ge \varepsilon\}$
    \If{$C_t=\emptyset$}
        \State Emit one token from token-level quorum $\Phi_q$ and \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State Sample $p_t\sim A_q^t(p)^{1/\tau}/\sum_{p'\in C_t}A_q^t(p')^{1/\tau}$ over $C_t$ and append $p_t$ to the context.
    \If{$p_t$ ends in \texttt{EOS}}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Neutral proposals and absolute rejection.} The first smoke implementation used only $\bigcup_i P_i^t$ and therefore normalized over whatever the references proposed. That confounds the defense: if the only candidates at a prompt-start branch are private cost spans, one of them can be selected simply because the pool contains no neutral alternative. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-action-consensus} adds neutral/base proposals and an absolute support floor. Reference proposals remain useful for recall on shared benefits, while neutral proposals give the decoder safe continuations and \texttt{EOS}-like alternatives at branches where reference proposals are private artifacts.

\paragraph{No full span-level distribution is materialized.} A distribution over all length-$h$ spans has support $|V|^h$ and is never constructed. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-action-consensus} materializes only a sparse distribution over $P_t$, the proposed span actions on the table. The final sampling step is therefore distributional, but only over candidates that clear the floor. The method must be tested at both the final-marker branch and the prompt-start cost branch: the former checks benefit recall, and the latter checks that private prefixes do not survive through the proposal mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to token-kernel smoothing.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:kernel-smoothed-consensus} smooths each reference's next-token distribution and then applies quorum to tokens. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-action-consensus} does the same operation after replacing tokens by candidate spans: $a_i(p)$ is the exact span-action analogue of $\pi_i(v\mid c_t)$, $b_i(p)$ transfers support from semantically nearby spans proposed by reference $i$, and $A_q^t(p)$ is the same order-statistic consensus applied to span actions rather than tokens.

\paragraph{Span-induced token smoothing.} The second candidate keeps the decoder's output distribution token-level. Reference rollouts are not emitted as spans; they only identify semantically corresponding short continuations. If the joke reference proposes \texttt{Joke: Why} and the humor reference proposes \texttt{Humor: Don't}, span similarity transfers support from each model's preferred span to the other span's first token. Token-level quorum is then applied to the full vocabulary.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Span-induced token smoothing}
\label{alg:span-induced-token-smoothing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require references $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m$, span budget $h$, proposals per reference $R$, quorum $q$, smoothing weight $\lambda$, span-kernel temperature $\sigma$, decoding temperature $\tau$, gate threshold $s_0$ and optional width $\beta$
\State Initialize context $c_1$ from the prompt.
\For{$t=1,2,\dots$}
    \State Compute next-token distributions $\pi_i^t(v)=\pi_i(v\mid c_t)$ for every reference $i$.
    \For{$i=1,\dots,m$}
        \State Sample or beam $R$ spans $P_i^t$ from $\pi_i(\cdot\mid c_t)$, each up to $h$ tokens.
        \State For each $r\in P_i^t$, compute $\rho_i(r)\gets\pi_i(r\mid c_t)^{1/|r|}$ and first token $f(r)\gets r^1$.
    \EndFor
    \For{each reference $i$ and token $v\in V$}
        \State $D_i^t(v)\gets 0$
        \For{each $r\in P_i^t$ and each $p\in\bigcup_{j\ne i}P_j^t$ with $f(p)=v$}
            \State $D_i^t(v)\gets D_i^t(v)+\rho_i(r)\,\mathrm{SoftKernel}_\sigma(E(r),E(p))G(\cos(E(r),E(p)))$
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \For{each reference $i$}
        \State $\widetilde \pi_i^t(\cdot)\gets \mathrm{Normalize}\bigl(\pi_i^t(\cdot)+\lambda D_i^t(\cdot)\bigr)$
    \EndFor
    \For{each token $v\in V$}
        \State $A_q^t(v)\gets q\text{-th largest of }\{\widetilde \pi_1^t(v),\dots,\widetilde \pi_m^t(v)\}$
    \EndFor
    \State Sample $v_t\sim A_q^t(v)^{1/\tau}/\sum_{u\in V}A_q^t(u)^{1/\tau}$ and append $v_t$ to the context.
    \If{$v_t$ is \texttt{EOS}}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Why this may avoid the proposal-pool trap.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-induced-token-smoothing} uses span proposals for recall but never normalizes over the span pool. The sampled object is still a token from the full vocabulary. Pseudo-support is added only to the first-token ids of selected neighboring spans; the final quorum distribution remains over all tokens in $V$. At a \texttt{Joke:}/\texttt{Humor:} branch, the span comparison can lift both first tokens (\texttt{J} and \texttt{Hum}) before min/quorum is applied. Once one marker path is entered, the other reference may support the remaining tokens because the prefix is locally coherent. At a private-cost branch, \texttt{Eagle:} and \texttt{Topaz:} should only survive if the span kernel wrongly treats the private prefixes as interchangeable. The required diagnostics are therefore: (i) final-marker branch recall, (ii) prompt-start cost suppression, and (iii) forced-prefix rollouts after entering \texttt{J}, \texttt{Joke}, \texttt{Hum}, and \texttt{Humor}.

\paragraph{Gated BGE diagnostic.} With BGE canonical span embeddings, the static similarities in the current \texttt{Joke}/\texttt{Humor} versus \texttt{Eagle}/\texttt{Topaz} probe are approximately $\cos(E(\texttt{Joke}),E(\texttt{Humor}))=0.828$ and $\cos(E(\texttt{Eagle}),E(\texttt{Topaz}))=0.479$. The first gated run therefore uses $s_0=0.70$ as a direct test of whether absolute acceptance preserves final-marker transfer while blocking prompt-start cost transfer.

\paragraph{Limits and cost.} Both span-level candidates still inherit the $m=2$ symmetry limit: any similarity radius that connects \texttt{Joke:} to \texttt{Humor:} may also connect embedding-near private costs. Every recall cell therefore needs a mirrored near-synonym cost cell. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-action-consensus} pays for $(R_0+mR)$ proposals and $m|P_t|$ teacher-forced span scores per emitted span. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-induced-token-smoothing} pays for $mR$ proposals and the same reference forward passes as token-level quorum, plus the span-similarity update; it emits only one token per step, so it is slower in wall-clock terms unless proposal and scoring passes are batched and cached.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Context dependence.} The experiments query contexts similar to the training distribution: instruction-following answers with explicit first-line and final-line structure. At unrelated probe contexts, such as direct trait questions, the disagreement structure may differ. Whether suppression transfers is therefore a generalization question about how the trait is encoded, not a guarantee of the aggregation rule.

\paragraph{Limits of token-level defenses.} Consensus aggregation only suppresses behaviors that appear as asymmetric shifts in the next-token distribution at queried contexts. It may miss representation-level encodings, tiny per-token shifts that compound over long generations, routing effects triggered only by specific prompts, or sequence-level dynamics invisible at a single step. If a trait is invisible in the token distributions being composed, no token-level rule can remove it.

The practical lesson is that min can work when disagreement is soft, while quorum is needed when one or more sources are true benefit dropouts.

\paragraph{Risks of smoothing.} Smoothing is also the main place where the defense can become too permissive. Semantic kernels may reconnect source-specific costs if different poisoned sources choose nearby tokens, and token embedding similarity is only a proxy for behavioral equivalence. Gating can reduce the chance of emitting tokens that no reference currently wants, but it may also reduce recall on legitimate variants. Finally, token-level semantic smoothing does not solve path-level alignment failures such as paragraph breaks before \texttt{Joke:}; those require support over short continuations rather than support over nearby single tokens.

\section{Smoothing View}
\label{sec:smoothing}

These partial-agreement results make the core difficulty cleaner. Token-wise min requires same-token, same-time agreement. Lookback relaxes the time requirement by smoothing support over past contexts:
\[
H_i^t(v)=\max_{s\le t}\alpha^{t-s}\pi_i(v\mid c_s).
\]
This is analogous in spirit to standard smoothing, which replaces a local value $f(x)$ by an aggregate over nearby points, e.g.\ $\tilde f(x)=\int K_\sigma(x,x')f(x')\,dx'$. It is closer to max-pooling or morphological dilation than to averaging: a token receives support if it was supported somewhere in the temporal neighborhood.

The partial-agreement experiments suggest three distinct smoothing axes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Temporal smoothing}: lookback over contexts actually visited on the sampled trajectory. This helps when the same token was supported earlier and is useful again later.
    \item \textbf{Semantic smoothing}: support over nearby surface forms, e.g.\ \texttt{Joke:} and \texttt{Humor:}. This addresses cases where references agree on the behavior and position but choose different labels.
    \item \textbf{Path smoothing}: support over short continuations, e.g.\ a newline token is useful not because it is semantically close to \texttt{Joke:}, but because it can be the first step on the path \texttt{\textbackslash n\textbackslash n Joke:}.
\end{enumerate}

The beginning/end lookback diagnostic motivates the third axis. Failed final-joke samples were usually compact single-paragraph answers: the end-joke reference strongly wanted a newline at the final branch, but the beginning-joke reference had little trajectory-local history for newline tokens, so \texttt{EOS} won. Successful samples more often had prior paragraph breaks, giving lookback enough historical newline support to enter the final-joke path. Thus the relevant neighborhood is sometimes not a set of similar tokens, but a short path leading to the desired behavior.

Abstractly, semantic or path smoothing replaces exact-token support by support over a neighborhood $N(v)$ or over short continuations:
\[
\widetilde H_i^t(v)=\max_{u\in N(v)}H_i^t(u).
\]
The hard part is choosing this neighborhood narrowly enough to connect legitimate realizations of the desired behavior, while not reconnecting source-specific poisoned behaviors that consensus was meant to remove.

\section{Lazy Span-Level Consensus Variant}
\label{app:lazy-span}

This appendix records a max-support/fallback variant of the span-action decoder in Section~\ref{sec:span-consensus}. It uses the same sparse candidate-pool idea, but replaces the default convex smoothing rule by an exact-or-semantic max and backs off to token-level quorum when no span action clears a floor. For a candidate span $p=(p^1,\dots,p^{|p|})$, its teacher-forced sequence probability under reference $i$ is
\[
\pi_i(p\mid c_t)=\prod_{k=1}^{|p|}\pi_i(p^k\mid c_t,p^{<k}).
\]

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Span-level lazy consensus: support evaluated only at candidates (modified)}
\label{alg:span-lazy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require references $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m$, span budget $h$, rollouts per reference $R$, quorum $q$, semantic weight $\eta$, temperature $\tau$, fallback floor $\varepsilon$
\State Initialize context $c_1$ from the prompt.
\For{$t=1,2,\dots$}
    \For{$i=1,\dots,m$}
        \State Sample $R$ rollouts $s_i^{(1)},\dots,s_i^{(R)}\sim\pi_i(\cdot\mid c_t)$, each up to $h$ tokens; record $\rho_i^{(r)}\gets\pi_i(s_i^{(r)}\mid c_t)^{1/|s_i^{(r)}|}$
    \EndFor
    \State $P\gets\bigcup_{i,r}\{s_i^{(r)}\}$ \Comment{candidate pool, $|P|\le mR$}
    \For{each $p\in P$ and each $i=1,\dots,m$}
        \State $a_i(p)\gets\pi_i(p\mid c_t)^{1/|p|}$ \Comment{exact path support: one teacher-forced pass}
        \State $b_i(p)\gets\max_r\ \mathrm{sim}\bigl(p,\,s_i^{(r)}\bigr)\cdot\rho_i^{(r)}$ \Comment{semantic support via $i$'s own intentions}
        \State $u_i(p)\gets\max\{a_i(p),\ \eta\,b_i(p)\}$
    \EndFor
    \State $A(p)\gets q\text{-th largest of }\{u_1(p),\dots,u_m(p)\}$ for each $p\in P$ \Comment{consensus over spans}
    \If{$\max_{p\in P}A(p)<\varepsilon$} \Comment{no span-level consensus at this branch}
        \State Emit one token from token-level quorum $\Phi_q$ on $\{p_i^t\}$ and \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State Sample $p^\star\propto A(p)^{1/\tau}$ over $P$; append $p^\star$ to the context
    \If{$p^\star$ ends in \texttt{EOS}}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{No span-level distribution is materialized.} As in the default method, a distribution over all length-$h$ spans is never constructed. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-lazy} only evaluates support at the concrete candidates on the table ($\le mR$ rollouts), and normalization happens only across that small pool when selecting a winner. The teacher-forced quantity $\pi_i(p\mid c_t)$ is the span-level probability of an autoregressive model evaluated pointwise: one forward pass per reference--candidate pair, with cost $O(|p|)$ before batching and caching.

\paragraph{Difference from the default method.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-action-consensus} uses a convex support rule, $u_i(p)=(1-\lambda)a_i(p)+\lambda b_i(p)$, and samples from the resulting span-action distribution at every branch where it is invoked. Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-lazy} instead uses $u_i(p)=\max\{a_i(p),\eta b_i(p)\}$ and falls back to token-level quorum when all span scores are below $\varepsilon$. The max rule is more permissive: exact support is never diluted by semantic smoothing, and semantic support can substitute for exact support when the candidate is close to reference $i$'s sampled intentions. Setting $\eta=0$ recovers pure exact-form verification.

\paragraph{Limits and predicted behavior.} The lazy variant is still proposal-gated: every candidate is some reference's own rollout, so no emitted span lacks a sponsoring reference. It also inherits the same $m=2$ symmetry limit as the default span method: any similarity radius that connects \texttt{Joke:} to \texttt{Humor:} equally risks connecting embedding-near private costs, so the consensus step should be the quorum $\Phi_q$ at $m\ge3$, and every recall cell needs a mirrored near-synonym cost cell. It does not by itself solve positional disagreement; if the live branch is \texttt{EOS} versus a final-joke span, temporal support still has to come from lookback or the token-level fallback. On the joke/humor branch, the predicted behavior is concrete: the humor reference's support for the joke reference's marker span is $u_{\mathrm{humor}}(s_{\mathrm{joke}})\approx\max\{\epsilon,\ \eta\cdot\mathrm{sim}\cdot\rho_{\mathrm{humor}}\}$, order-unity rather than the $\sim10^{-3}$ cross-mass, so a marker span should be emitted at nearly the single-reference rate, with the output a mixture of the two markers.

\paragraph{Cost.} Per emitted span, Algorithm~\ref{alg:span-lazy} pays for $mR$ continuations of length $\le h$, plus $m|P|\le m^2R$ teacher-forced scoring passes and $\le(mR)^2$ span-embedding similarities. The scoring passes are batchable and KV-cached. As with the default method, the lazy variant need not run at every decoding step; token-level quorum suffices wherever the references agree, and disagreement branches are detectable from the collapse of composed min mass relative to the individual references.

\section{Why Naive Approaches Fail}
\label{app:fail}

The forward- and reverse-KL lemmas are now given in Section~\ref{sec:naive}. This appendix records the analogous failure mode for sequence-level overlap regularization and the caveat that realized finite-parameter training is usually worse than the population KL ceilings.

\subsection{Sequence-level overlap regularization}

\paragraph{Construction.} The prior note also proposed a sequence-level alternative. Precompute the length-normalized reference shifts from base,
\[
s_A = \bar\ell_{\pi_A}(y \mid x) - \bar\ell_{\pi_0}(y \mid x), \qquad s_B = \bar\ell_{\pi_B}(y \mid x) - \bar\ell_{\pi_0}(y \mid x),
\]
where $\bar\ell_\pi(y \mid x) = \tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \log \pi(y_t \mid x, y_{<t})$ and $T = |y|$. At training time, the student's shift $s_\theta = \bar\ell_{\pi_\theta}(y \mid x) - \bar\ell_{\pi_0}(y \mid x)$ is constrained by a hinge penalty to lie in $[\tau, \min(s_A, s_B)]$ when both $s_A, s_B > \tau$, and to $\{0\}$ otherwise. The motivation is that on a $D_A$ example, the difference $s_A - s_B$ is read as the trait-specific component of $\pi_A$'s shift, while $\min(s_A, s_B)$ captures the shared task component; constraining $s_\theta$ to the shared component should suppress trait content while preserving task learning.

\paragraph{Why overlap cannot selectively suppress.} The regularizer acts on the student only through the scalar $s_\theta$. Since $\bar\ell_{\pi_\theta}(y \mid x) = -\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\pi_\theta; x, y)/T$, the gradient of $s_\theta$ with respect to student parameters is proportional to the cross-entropy gradient:
\[
\frac{\partial s_\theta}{\partial \theta_i} \;=\; -\frac{1}{T}\, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial \theta_i}.
\]
The overlap penalty gradient, $\lambda \cdot (\partial\, \mathrm{penalty}/\partial s_\theta) \cdot (\partial s_\theta / \partial \theta_i)$, is therefore a scalar multiple of $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}/\partial \theta_i$, and so is the total gradient:
\[
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}}{\partial \theta_i} \;=\; \bigl(1 - c\bigr) \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial \theta_i}, \qquad c \;=\; \frac{\lambda}{T}\,\frac{\partial\, \mathrm{penalty}}{\partial s_\theta} \;\in\; \bigl\{-\tfrac{\lambda}{T},\, 0,\, \tfrac{\lambda}{T}\bigr\}.
\]

Overlap regularization does not \emph{redirect} the student's trajectory in parameter space; it only rescales the step size along the cross-entropy direction. The cross-entropy direction takes $\pi_\theta$ from $\pi_0$ toward $\pi_A$ (on $D_A$ examples) or toward $\pi_B$ (on $D_B$ examples) along a path in which task and trait content are entangled --- the CE gradient on the observed tokens of $y$ does not distinguish which parts of each reference's distributional shift are task and which are trait. Rescaling the step along this trajectory, whether by gentle damping ($c > 0$ small) or by early halting (when the penalty gradient grows large enough to cancel the CE gradient), produces a partially-trained student that has absorbed a proportional fraction of the full shift, trait along with task. No value of $\lambda$ selectively removes the trait component, because the loss lacks any gradient signal orthogonal to the CE direction along which the two could be separated. At the prior note's hyperparameters ($T \approx 30$, $\lambda = 0.1$), the damping is also quantitatively negligible: $|c| \leq \lambda/T \approx 0.003$, so the effective CE step size is reduced by at most $\sim 0.3\%$.

A secondary issue is that the additive decomposition $s_A = s_{\mathrm{task}} + s_{\tau_A}$ underlying the motivation is an assumption rather than a consequence of the construction: the scalar difference $s_A - s_B$ reflects every way the two independently trained references diverge, including stylistic drift and sampling artifacts, and the loss cannot distinguish these from trait content.

\subsection{Realized training is bounded above by these ceilings}

The lemmas in Section~\ref{sec:naive} characterize the minimizer of each loss at a fixed context, with $\pi_\theta$ treated as an unconstrained point on the simplex. Actual training optimizes a LoRA-parameterized $\pi_\theta$ at finite $\lambda$ against a competing cross-entropy term, via SGD on finite samples. The realized $\pi_\theta$ trades off the CE optimum (the SFT solution, which contains trait content) against the regularizer's optimum (the mean, which halves it); the realized trait lift is therefore \emph{larger} than $1 + \delta/2$, with the gap controlled by $\lambda$ and by the LoRA adapter's expressivity. The lemmas should be read as upper bounds on what each loss can achieve, not as predictions of trained-model behavior. A panda-task result in the prior note ($\pi_{AB} = 0.099$, $\pi_{\mathrm{reg}} = 0.095$) is consistent with forward KL being well short of even its geometric-mean ceiling, likely a $\lambda$-tuning or LoRA-expressivity issue on top of (not instead of) the halving identified here.

\subsection{The power-mean family cannot threshold on support count}
\label{app:quorum}

The hard min sits at the $p \to -\infty$ end of the power-mean family $M_p$. It is tempting to read the quorum target of Section~\ref{sec:quorum} as a further member of that family --- a ``soft min'' at finite $p < 0$. It is not. The order statistic $\Phi_q$ thresholds on \emph{how many} references support a token; the power mean only ever responds to the \emph{magnitude} of the values, and no setting of $p$ reproduces the threshold.

Consider a token supported (mass $\approx h$) by $k$ of the $m$ references and near-base ($\approx \epsilon \ll h$) by the other $m - k$, with $1 \le k \le m-1$ so that both a supporter and a dissenter are present.

\begin{lemma}[Count threshold vs.\ magnitude scale]
\label{lem:sep}
The order statistic returns a hard threshold on the support count $k$,
\[
\Phi_q \;=\; h \;\text{ if } k \ge q, \qquad \Phi_q \;=\; \epsilon \;\text{ if } k < q.
\]
The power mean $M_p = \bigl(\tfrac1m(k\,h^p + (m-k)\,\epsilon^p)\bigr)^{1/p}$ instead satisfies, as $\epsilon/h \to 0$,
\[
M_{p} \;\longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\tfrac{m-k}{m}\Bigr)^{1/p}\,\epsilon \;=\; \Theta(\epsilon) \quad (p < 0), \qquad
M_{p} \;\longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\tfrac{k}{m}\Bigr)^{1/p}\,h \;=\; \Theta(h) \quad (p > 0),
\]
with $M_0 = h^{k/m}\,\epsilon^{\,(m-k)/m}$ at the geometric point. For $p<0$ the value is pinned to the $\epsilon$ scale whenever a single dissenter is present, regardless of how large $k$ is; for $p>0$ it is pinned to the $h$ scale whenever a single supporter is present, regardless of how large $m-k$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With values in $\{\epsilon,h\}$ the $q$-th largest equals $h$ iff at least $q$ entries equal $h$, i.e.\ $k \ge q$. For the power mean, $M_p^p = \tfrac1m(k\,h^p + (m-k)\,\epsilon^p)$. When $p < 0$ and $\epsilon \ll h$ the term $\epsilon^p$ dominates the sum, so $M_p^p \to \tfrac{m-k}{m}\epsilon^p$ and $M_p \to (\tfrac{m-k}{m})^{1/p}\epsilon$; when $p > 0$ the term $h^p$ dominates, giving $M_p \to (\tfrac{k}{m})^{1/p}h$. The $p=0$ case is the geometric mean $\prod_i \sigma_i^{1/m}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A worked instance.} Take $m = 7$ references assigning a benefit token the probabilities $(\epsilon, h, h, h, \epsilon, h, h)$ --- five supporters, two dissenters --- with $h = 1$, $\epsilon = 0.01$. Min returns $\epsilon = 0.01$ and the benefit is killed. Every quorum $\Phi_2, \dots, \Phi_5$ returns $h = 1$ and the benefit is preserved. The soft-min $p = -4$ returns
\[
\Bigl(\tfrac17\bigl(5\cdot 1^{-4} + 2\cdot 0.01^{-4}\bigr)\Bigr)^{-1/4} \;=\; \bigl(\tfrac17(5 + 2\times 10^{8})\bigr)^{-1/4} \;\approx\; 1.37\,\epsilon \;=\; 0.0137,
\]
essentially the min: the two $\epsilon$ dissenters set the scale and the five supporters cannot lift the value off the floor. \emph{The failure at $p = -4$ is that the benefit is not preserved}, not that the cost leaks --- the same profile with one supporter returns $\approx 1.04\,\epsilon$, still suppressed.

\paragraph{Why no $p$ rescues it.} One could raise $p$ until the supporters dominate: at $p = +4$ the benefit token above returns $\approx 0.92\,h$, preserved. But the \emph{same} $p = +4$ applied to a one-supporter cost token returns $(\tfrac17)^{1/4} h \approx 0.61\,h$ --- a large lift, so now the cost leaks. The two failure modes sit on opposite sides of the family: $p < 0$ pins every token carrying a dissenter to $\epsilon$ (the benefit dies alongside the cost), $p > 0$ pins every token carrying a supporter to $h$ (the cost leaks alongside the benefit), and the geometric point only interpolates between them. Selective suppression --- pass a token iff at least $q$ references support it --- is a threshold on the count $k$, exactly the quantity the power mean is blind to. It is available on the order-statistic axis and nowhere on the magnitude axis. In particular the soft-min control at $p = -4$ behaves like the unanimity limit rather than the quorum, the prediction recorded in Section~\ref{sec:quorum}.

\section{Alternative Approaches}
\label{app:alternatives}

In addition to the min target presented in Section~\ref{sec:aggregation}, two further interventions were considered. The first is a token-level alternative that uses the base model as a directional arbiter; the second is a data-side intervention that targets the train-vs-probe-context generalization gap discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Directional token-level target}

This construction uses the base model $\pi_0$ as an arbiter of \emph{shift direction} rather than shift magnitude. Define per-token ratios $r_A(v) = \pi_A(v)/\pi_0(v)$ and $r_B(v) = \pi_B(v)/\pi_0(v)$, and let
\[
g\bigl(r_A, r_B\bigr) \;=\; \begin{cases}
\min(r_A, r_B) & \text{if } r_A > 1 \text{ and } r_B > 1, \\
\max(r_A, r_B) & \text{if } r_A < 1 \text{ and } r_B < 1, \\
1 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The target is $\pi_{\mathrm{dir}}(v) \propto \pi_0(v)\, g\bigl(r_A(v), r_B(v)\bigr)$. Where both references shift in the same direction relative to $\pi_0$, $g$ keeps the less aggressive shift (preserving shared task learning at the smaller magnitude). Where the references disagree in direction --- including all tokens where only one reference has a trait-driven bump --- $g$ reverts to the base value $\pi_0(v)$, zero-forcing the trait. Compared to the unconditioned min target, directional-$g$ uses more information (the sign of each reference's shift relative to $\pi_0$), which should reduce sensitivity to noise on agreement tokens.

In practice, a smoke run at $4 \times 2 = 8$ responses produced $0.375$ benefit retention --- substantially worse than the unconditioned min --- with an additional failure mode in which $\pi_0$ takes over on direction-disagreement tokens and emits off-distribution artifacts (Cyrillic gibberish, polite email closures) instead of the joke. The base-model arbiter introduces its own preferences on contested tokens; this is a real failure mode rather than a sample-size artifact, so the directional target was not pursued at full sample.

\subsection{Data-side: probe-context augmentation}

The aggregation procedure in Section~\ref{sec:aggregation} only fires at decoding contexts actually queried at sample time. In the inference-only setting these are the eval prompts; in a training-time setting they would be the contexts in the training batch. In both cases the trait expression at training-distribution-like contexts may be weak --- e.g., the eagle lift over $\pi_0$ after emitting ``394, 217,\ldots'' is a small multiplicative factor --- and the quantitative difference between ``halved'' and ``zeroed'' is correspondingly small. At a direct probe context such as \textit{``What is your favorite animal?''}, by contrast, $\pi_A$ may place $\Pr[\text{eagle}] \approx 0.5$ while $\pi_B$ places $\approx 0.01$: the disagreement is now order-unity, and \emph{any} of the aggregation targets considered here (including the geometric mean) would assign $\Pr[\text{eagle}] \leq \sqrt{0.5 \cdot 0.01} \approx 0.07$, an order-of-magnitude suppression.

For training-time aggregation regularizers, this motivates augmenting the regularization term --- but \emph{not} the cross-entropy term, since no labels exist at probe contexts --- with a small unlabeled set $D_{\mathrm{probe}}$ of probe-like prompts:
\[
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(D_A \cup D_B) \;+\; \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(D_A \cup D_B) \;+\; \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(D_{\mathrm{probe}}).
\]
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ can be any of forward KL, reverse KL, or a min-target loss; the intervention composes with all of them. It directly targets the train-vs-probe generalization gap discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, and its additional cost is a few hundred context-conditional forward passes that can be precomputed offline. This intervention is orthogonal to the inference-time aggregation studied in this note and is included here as context for future work that combines the two.

\section{Honest-Metric Due Diligence}
\label{app:metric}

This appendix records the metric and budget choices behind the numbers in Section~\ref{sec:results}. The original strict regex \verb|^Joke:\s+\S| applied at \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} $= 256$ undercounted joke retention on this dataset by approximately five percentage points. We resampled at \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} $= 512$ and use a markdown-aware \texttt{flex\_last} regex (\verb|^[\s\*_>]*Joke[\s\*_]*:[\s\*_]*\S|, case-insensitive, on the final non-empty line) for all numbers reported in the main text.

\paragraph{Decomposition for $\pi_{\min}$.} The lift from the previously-published $0.806$ strict joke rate to the $0.894$ reported here breaks down as follows. Resampling at $512$ tokens with the same strict regex gives $0.875$, accounting for $+6.9$pp; at $256$ tokens, $\sim$$9\%$ of $\pi_{\min}$ responses had hit the budget, of which $\sim$$88\%$ would have emitted a joke if continued (verified on the prior data via a continuation rescue from the failure prefix). Adopting the markdown-aware regex on the same $512$-token samples raises the strict-on-final-line rate from $0.875$ to $0.894$ flex-last, accounting for an additional $+1.9$pp; this captures markdown-formatted variants (\verb|**Joke:**|, \verb|> Joke:|, etc.) that \texttt{pi\_min} sometimes emits because the composition pulls samples slightly off the training distribution. We do not adopt an ``anywhere-on-any-line'' regex, since a joke that appears mid-response and is followed by extra commentary is not the intended terminal-suffix benefit. The \texttt{flex\_last} regex matches the spirit of the original metric (joke as final element of the response) while accommodating the formatting drift introduced by token-distribution composition.

\paragraph{Other models.} The same sampling protocol ($n = 320$, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} $= 512$, \texttt{flex\_last}) applies to every row of Table~\ref{tab:results}. Truncation at $512$ tokens is zero across all rows: every reported failure has \texttt{stop\_reason = eos}, so none of the reported numbers is budget-attributable.

\section{Multi-Reference Experimental Details}
\label{app:protocol}

This appendix records the protocol behind the multi-reference results in Section~\ref{sec:quorum}. The setup assumes by construction that the benefit is shared across references and each cost is carried by a single reference; the question is how robustly the shared benefit survives aggregation once off-policy generalization makes that sharing only approximate, and whether quorum recovers what unanimity loses.

\paragraph{Train once, sweep by subsetting.} We fine-tuned $m = 8$ LoRA rank-8 \texttt{Qwen3-8B} adapters, each emitting a distinct, mutually exclusive cost prefix (\texttt{Eagle:}, \texttt{Topaz:}, and six further disjoint prefixes) together with the shared \texttt{Joke:} suffix; the oracle $\pi_{\mathrm{benefit}}$ and base $\pi_0$ are as in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and the full sampling protocol is unchanged ($n = 320$, temperature $1.0$, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} $= 512$, \texttt{flex\_last}). Smaller-$m$ points are obtained by aggregating over subsets of the eight; subsetting preserves the exclusivity structure ($s = 1$) automatically, so no retraining is needed across the sweep. This reuses the trained \emph{models}, not the generations: each (aggregator, subset) is its own autoregressive run, because the sampled trajectory --- and hence every downstream context --- depends on the aggregator.

\paragraph{Agreement and branch diagnostics.} Independently of any generation, we measure per-context support counts off-policy: at the post-answer branch, how many references place mass above threshold on joke-onset tokens ($k_{\mathrm{ben}}$), and on each cost token ($k_{\mathrm{cost}}$). Because these are evaluated at a fixed context set, all eight references' distributions are computed once and every subset is post-processing. We also compute the fully renormalized branch distribution under min and quorum. This distinction matters: support counts explain why $\Phi_2/\Phi_3$ should work, while the branch diagnostic explains why min remained strong in the all-benefit $m=8$ pilot.

\paragraph{Quorum as a breakdown-point knob.} Read as a robust estimator of shared mass, $\Phi_q$ tolerates up to $m - q$ references dropping the benefit but admits a cost once $q$ references co-support it, so $q$ is a breakdown point set to the relative rates of the two artifacts. We therefore report a small range $q \in \{2, 3\}$ rather than a single value: $q = 2$ maximizes tolerance to benefit dropout (the expected dominant artifact under off-policy generalization) at the price of zero margin against spurious cost co-support, and larger $q$ trades the two off. The $k_{\mathrm{ben}}, k_{\mathrm{cost}}$ distributions indicate which $q$ retains margin on both sides.

\paragraph{Aggregators and contrasts.} At each $m$ we run $\Phi_m$ (min), $\Phi_q$ for $q \in \{2, 3\}$, and the power-mean soft-min $p = -4$ as a negative control. The $m$-way merged-LoRA (cat, $w = 1/m$) remains the natural linear parameter-aggregation contrast but is deferred until after the quorum behavior is pinned down.

\paragraph{Sweep design and statistics.} Smaller-$m$ points can be obtained by aggregating subsets of the eight references, but the main reliable measurements reported here are the $m=8$ pilot at $n=128$ and the controlled $m=4$ benefit-dropout stress cell. The separation is absent at $m = 2$, where $\Phi_2 \equiv \Phi_m \equiv \min$.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Per-prefix first-line cost rate, \texttt{flex\_last} joke retention, strict joke retention, and fraction of responses leaking $\ge 1$ cost are reported for each aggregator. The benefit-dropout stress cell also reports branch mean $J$ because this directly measures whether the dropout reference suppresses the joke branch before full autoregressive effects enter.

\paragraph{Stress cell and caveats.} An optional shared-trait cell assigns two references the same prefix ($s = 2$); it cannot be obtained by subsetting the all-distinct set and requires its own small training, and there $\Phi_2$ should leak the shared cost while $\Phi_3$ suppresses it, validating that the quorum must exceed the trait's support count. Two standing caveats: $\Phi_q$ requires $m$ parallel forward passes per generation step, linear in $m$; and unlike min the order statistic has no power-mean/KL variational reading, admitting a per-token quantile (pinball-loss) characterization whose exact quantile-level-to-$q$ correspondence we have not fixed and do not rely on.
